The UGC Accelerator Roadmap

Eight days from nothing to a paid brand deal. Not theory — a list of things to do, in order, each one finishing with something shipped. Tick them off as you go; this page remembers where you got to.

The end goal

Write these down before you start. A roadmap without a destination is just a to-do list.

Financially: $______/month by __/__/____

Personally:   — the actual reason. Not "more money." What the money is for.

There's one rule

Ship on the day it says. Every task below ends in something that exists — a video, an application, a portfolio. Not something you watched or read.

Slipping a day is fine. Stopping is not. If you fall behind, pick up at the next task rather than restarting — the order matters more than the dates.

Day numbers are relative to whenever you start. Once your first deal lands, the clock stops mattering: you work to the brand's schedule from then on, because you don't control when brands reply.

The roadmap

Work top to bottom. Don't skip ahead to the money — the early days are what make the later ones work.

01
Pick your lane

Ends with: a niche chosen and three creators saved.

✓
Choose one niche and write it down
Fitness, skincare, study tools, finance apps, food, pets — one. Brands hire creators who look like they already belong in the category. "General lifestyle" is not a category anyone is hiring for.
✓
Find 3 creators already getting paid in that niche
Search the niche on TikTok and Instagram and look for videos with a brand in them. Save their profiles. These are your reference set for everything that follows — you will come back to them constantly.
✓
Save 10 of their videos that got unusually high views
Not their average — their outliers. A video doing 10x the account's normal is telling you the format worked, not that the creator is popular. Those formats are what you'll rebuild.
02
Learn the anatomy of a video that works

Ends with: one saved video broken into its four beats, in writing.

✓
Transcribe one of your saved videos, word for word
By hand. It's tedious and that's the point — typing it out is what makes you notice how little is actually said, and how fast the first line arrives.
✓
Split that transcript into four beats
Hook — the first line, which exists only to buy the second. Intention — what they're promising you'll get. Body — the actual content, usually shorter than you'd guess. Lesson — the line that makes it feel worth having watched. Almost every video that works has all four, in that order.
✓
Rewrite each beat as a fill-in-the-blank template
Strip out their specifics and leave the shape: "You're going to be the [identity] that [outcome] every [timeframe]." That skeleton is what transfers to your own story. Their numbers and credentials don't.
03
Make your first video ship it

Ends with: one finished video on your camera roll.

✓
Write your script into yesterday's template
Your story, their structure. If a line only worked because that creator had a number you don't have, replace it with something equally true about you. Borrowing a claim you haven't earned is the single fastest way to look fake.
✓
Film it in one sitting. Ten takes maximum
Natural light, phone at eye height, quiet room. The first video is not going to be good and doesn't need to be — the point is having made one. Nobody's first is good.
✓
Cut every pause, then watch it once on mute
Dead air is what kills retention. Watching on mute tells you whether the visuals hold on their own — if it's boring silent, it's boring with sound.
04
Get obsessive about the first three seconds

Ends with: five hooks written, one reshot.

✓
Write five different openings for the video you just made
Same video, five first lines. A question, a claim, a number, a contradiction, a confession. Most of your result comes from this one line, so five minutes here beats an hour on the edit.
✓
Check each one opens a loop
A working hook plants a question the viewer needs answered and doesn't answer it yet. "I made $2k this month" closes the loop. "The mistake that cost me my first brand deal" keeps it open.
✓
Reshoot the opening with your best one
Just the opening. You now have two versions of one video, which is the cheapest experiment in this whole document.
05
Build the portfolio ship it

Ends with: a link you can send to a brand.

✓
Make two more videos, different formats
If the first was talking-to-camera, make a demo and a voiceover-over-b-roll. Three videos in three formats says "I can do what you need." Three near-identical ones say "I can do one thing."
✓
Put all three on one page
A Google Drive folder, a Notion page, a free one-page site — it genuinely does not matter. Brands click the link, watch ten seconds of each, and decide. Nobody has ever lost a deal over portfolio design.
✓
Add one line at the top: niche, formats, turnaround
"Skincare UGC — demos and talking-head. 3-day turnaround." That's it. Brands are scanning for fit and speed, not reading a bio.
06
Understand what you're actually being paid money

Ends with: knowing what a good offer looks like before you're offered one.

✓
Learn the three ways UGC deals pay
Flat per video — simplest, usually $20–$80 starting out. CPM — paid per 1,000 views, so a video that travels pays more. Base plus view tiers — a floor, then bonuses at 10k, 100k, 1M. The most common shape for the good ones.
✓
Work out what a deal is worth at your realistic volume
Base rate times how many you can genuinely post in a month. Do not count the top view-bonus tier — those aren't repeatable, and a deal that only pays if you go viral isn't a deal, it's a lottery ticket.
✓
Decide your floor
The number below which you say no. Write it down now, while nobody is offering you anything — it's much harder to decide once there's an actual offer on the table.
07
Start applying money

Ends with: 20 applications sent.

✓
Build a list of 40 brands in your niche
Brands already running creator content — you'll see them in the videos you saved on day 1. If a brand has never posted creator content, you're selling them the concept and the deal at once. Much harder.
✓
Write one pitch, three sentences long
Who you are and your niche. What you noticed about their content. Your portfolio link. That's the whole email. Long pitches read as desperate and nobody finishes them.
✓
Send 20 today
Twenty. Not three carefully agonised ones. This is a volume game early — the reply rate is low and there is nothing wrong with you when most don't answer.
✓
Apply to two creator platforms as well
Platforms that pool brand campaigns will often get you a first paid deal faster than cold outreach, because the brand has already decided to hire someone. Search "UGC creator platform" and pick two with real campaigns listed.
08
Make volume survivable

Ends with: a repeatable week you can actually sustain.

✓
Turn your best video into three variations
Same script, different opening and different footage. Variations are how one good idea becomes a week of posting. Reshoot the footage rather than just re-cutting it — new raw footage outperforms a speed change every time.
✓
Batch: one filming day, one editing day
Setting up your camera five times a week is what makes people quit. Film everything in one session, edit in another. It's the same output for a fraction of the friction.
✓
Write down what worked, in one paragraph
Which hook, which format, which brand replied. Eight days in you have real data about your own audience, and it beats anybody's general advice — including everything above.
↻
Then, on repeat

No day numbers from here. This is the loop.

✓
Max out the deal you have before chasing the next
One brand you post for consistently is worth more than four you did one video for. The second month with the same brand is nearly always easier and better paid than the first.
✓
Keep a running file of formats that worked
Every time something outperforms, write down why in two lines. That file becomes the thing you work from — and eventually the thing you hand an editor.
✓
Hand off editing once you're posting daily
The moment editing is what's stopping you posting, it's the thing to give away first. Write down how you cut your videos before you hire anyone — an editor without a spec produces work you'll redo yourself.
✓
Raise your rate every time you can point at a result
A video that did well is the entire argument. You don't need a reason beyond "this performed, my rate is now X."

The video checklist

Run this before you send or post anything. It catches most of what gets a video rejected.

1
The first line lands within one second. No logo, no intro, no "hey guys."
2
The hook opens a question and doesn't answer it yet.
3
Every pause is cut. Watch at 1x and flinch at the dead air.
4
Captions are on, and no more than 3–4 words on screen at once.
5
The visual changes at least every 3 seconds — angle, b-roll, or framing.
6
Audio is level throughout. No section noticeably quieter than another.
7
The product is visible and in focus for at least 3 continuous seconds.
8
It's shot vertical, 9:16, and nothing important sits under the caption bar.
9
It ends on the last spoken word. No outro, no sign-off, no fade.
10
You watched it once on mute and it still made sense.
